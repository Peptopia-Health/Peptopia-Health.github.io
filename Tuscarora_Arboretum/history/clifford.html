J. R. Clifford

West Virginia’s first African American attorney — teacher, newspaper founder, and civil rights pioneer of Martinsburg.

John Robert “J. R.” Clifford

September 13, 1848 – October 6, 1933 · Martinsburg, West Virginia

John Robert “J. R.” Clifford (September 13, 1848 – October 6, 1933) was a Civil War veteran, teacher, newspaper publisher, and West Virginia’s first African American attorney. Born free in what is now Grant County, he came to Martinsburg as a teacher and principal of the Sumner School, studied law, and in 1887 became the first African American admitted to practice before the West Virginia Supreme Court of Appeals. From Martinsburg he published the Pioneer Press (1882–1917), the state’s first African American newspaper. In 1898 he won the landmark Carrie Williams case, in which the state Supreme Court held that discrimination “because of color alone” is unconstitutional — more than half a century before Brown v. Board of Education. With W. E. B. Du Bois he co-founded the Niagara Movement, forerunner of the NAACP, and chaired the arrangements for its 1906 meeting at Harpers Ferry. He practiced law in Martinsburg until his death at 85 and now rests at Arlington National Cemetery.
