History of Tuscarora Creek in Martinsburg, WV (1700s)

Tuscarora Creek, running through Berkeley County near Martinsburg, was a key geographic and economic feature in the 18th century, supporting early settlement, agriculture, and milling.

Early settlement and land use

By the 1740s, the area along Tuscarora Creek was settled by Joseph and John Morgan, who came from Pennsylvania. They established farms and small enterprises along the waterway, taking advantage of the creek’s flow for milling and other purposes. The creek’s location made it a natural hub for early community life and trade.

Industrial development in the 1760s

In 1765, General Adam Stephen, who later became the founder of Martinsburg in 1778, set up gristmills and flaxseed-oil mills along the creek. Patterson’s Mill, built in 1765, is one of the earliest known structures in the Tuscarora Creek Historic District. These mills were central to the local economy, processing grain and flax for both local use and export.

Settlement patterns and community growth

The creek’s water power and fertile floodplains attracted settlers, leading to the development of small villages and farms. The area became known for its agricultural productivity and artisanal work, such as flax processing, which was important in the colonial economy.

Cultural and social significance

By the late 18th century, the creek area was also a site of social and religious life. The Tuscarora Church (1802) and other community buildings later emerged in the same general area. The creek’s presence shaped the layout of early Martinsburg and surrounding settlements, and it remained a focal point for economic and social activity well into the 19th century.

Historic preservation

Today, the Tuscarora Creek Historic District, listed on the National Register of Historic Places in 1980, preserves many of these early structures and sites, including Patterson’s Mill, Elm Dale, and the Mong House. These remain evidence of the creek’s role in the region’s 18th-century history.

In summary, during the 1700s, Tuscarora Creek was a vital resource for early settlers in Martinsburg, supporting agriculture, milling, and community life, and it laid the foundation for the area’s economic and cultural development.

History Topics

J. R. Clifford

Martinsburg’s civil rights pioneer — West Virginia’s first African American attorney, founder of the Pioneer Press, and co-founder of the Niagara Movement. He made his home in Martinsburg for more than 50 years.

The Roundhouse & the Great Strike

The B&O shops rose along the banks of Tuscarora Creek in 1866 — the West Roundhouse is the oldest fully covered roundhouse in America. On July 16, 1877, the Great Railroad Strike ignited here and spread across the nation.
