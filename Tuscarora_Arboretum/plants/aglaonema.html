Aglaonema

Aglaonema spp. — the Chinese Evergreens

The low-light champion of the houseplant world — broad, lance-shaped leaves painted in silver, cream, pink, and red over deep green, from a plant that evolved on rainforest floors where only 1–5% of sunlight ever lands. Cultivated in Asia for centuries as a good-luck plant, and tough enough to thrive for decades indoors.

Leaves rise on stalks from a central crown, each blade broad and lance-shaped with the pattern laid along the midrib — silver centers with green margins in the classics, pink and red washes in the modern Thai-bred types. The flowers are quiet aroid affairs: a pale spathe hood around a golden spadix. Kin to peace lily, pothos, and philodendron.

🏆 The Florida chapter: ‘Silver Bay’ — the big silver-centered plant in every office in America — is an interspecific hybrid released in 1992 by the University of Florida’s IFAS breeding program at Apopka. ‘Silver Queen’ defined the category for decades before it.

🎛️ Conditions — Measured

Silver-and-green types genuinely tolerate low light — a dark corner is fine. The red and pink cultivars are the exception: give them medium-bright light or their color quietly reverts to plain green. Direct sun scorches any of them.

📋 The Care Card

WaterWhen the top 1–2 inches are dry, water deeply and drain fully. Overwatering is the #1 killer — soggy soil rots the roots. Never let it sit in a full saucer.
Water qualitySensitive to fluoride and chlorine — they burn the leaf tips brown over time. Use filtered, distilled, or rainwater, or tap water left out overnight. Flush the pot every few months.
ColdThe failure mode to fear. Chilling injury starts below ~59°F and can show up days later — UF/IFAS found ‘Silver Queen’ lost 30% of its leaf area after one day at 55°F. Keep it off cold windowsills and away from winter drafts and AC blasts.
SoilRich, well-draining aroid mix — potting soil with perlite and bark, pH 6.0–7.5. Snug pot: one size up at a time.
FeedingBalanced feed, diluted, every 4–6 weeks in spring and summer only.
UpkeepWipe dust from the broad leaves; snip yellowing leaves at the base. Basal offshoots make the plant fuller with age.

✂️ Propagation — Division First

  1. Division (easiest): in spring, unpot a mature clump and tease the basal shoots apart so each piece keeps its own roots; pot each up fresh.
  2. Stem cuttings: cut 4–6 inches of healthy stem just below a node, keeping at least 2 nodes.
  3. Set into moist, free-draining mix (rooting gel optional) and keep it warm — 72–76°F; roots in about 3–5 weeks.
  4. Cultivars are sterile hybrids — they never come true from seed, so cuttings and division are the only roads.

🩺 Troubleshooting

Brown leaf tipsFluoride/chlorine in tap water, or dry air — switch water, raise humidity.
Yellowing leavesOverwatering first suspect — check the soil and roots; also natural aging at the base.
Red/pink fading to greenToo little light for a color cultivar — move brighter, still indirect.
Sudden collapse after a cold nightChilling injury — move somewhere warm; damaged leaves won’t recover, new growth will.
White cottony clustersMealybugs (its main pest, with scale and spider mites) — wipe with alcohol, treat.
🐈

Keep out of Weenie’s reach. Unlike the waffle plant, Aglaonema is toxic to cats and dogs — insoluble calcium oxalate crystals in every part cause mouth pain, drooling, and vomiting if chewed. Shelf it high or hang it. If she chews it: ASPCA Poison Control (888) 426-4435.

👨‍👩‍👧 The Family Album — Four to Know

‘Silver Bay’broad silver centers, deep green margins — the 1992 Florida classic
‘Silver Queen’heavy silver striping over green — defined the category for decades
‘Siam Aurora’deep green centers lit by red-pink margins and veins
‘Red Valentine’burgundy-pink new growth maturing to olive-green

Some two dozen species and hundreds of cultivars deep, the genus runs from the plain green A. modestum — the original Chinese evergreen — to Thai-bred plants splashed like crotons. Nearly all of them share the same easy temperament: shade-tolerant, slow, and long-lived.
