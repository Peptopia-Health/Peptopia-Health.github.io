Plants

The living collection — plants grown, studied, and written up one at a time. Every plant gets a researched guide with its true name, its story, and exactly how to keep it thriving.

Ginkgo

Ginkgo biloba · Maidenhair Tree · Ginkgoaceae

The last living species of an order older than the dinosaurs, told through Blandy Experimental Farm’s famous Ginkgo Grove — 300+ seed-grown trees descended from one unnamed Charlottesville mother, a fifty-year experiment that answered its question after its asker died, a dying side being healed with bald cypress, and the embossed tags that keep every tree’s story. With a full teaching narration, a spoken note for every photograph, and the Pratt Ginkgo correction — the famous Rotunda tree is male, and never bore a seed.

Snow White Waffle Plant

Strobilanthes alternata ‘Snow White’ · Waffle Plant · Acanthaceae

A variegated waffle plant from the humid undergrowth of Java and Malaysia — crinkled, quilted leaves of light green splashed with white, edged in pink, over a vivid magenta-purple underside. It is a seedling of the purple ‘Exotica’ waffle plant, selected for its heavy white variegation. The full guide covers its identification, its botany, and the moist, steamy care it loves.

Aglaonema

Aglaonema spp. · Chinese Evergreen · Araceae

The low-light champion of the houseplant world, from rainforest floors in Southeast Asia where almost no direct sun ever lands. Broad leaves come painted in silver, cream, pink, and red across some two dozen species and hundreds of cultivars — and it has been kept for centuries as a good-luck plant. The full guide covers its care, its cold sensitivity, and one important caution: it is toxic to pets if chewed, so it lives up high, out of the cat’s reach.

Coral Cactus

Euphorbia lactea ‘Cristata’ · Crested Euphorbia · Euphorbiaceae

Not a cactus at all — a crested, variegated euphorbia whose growing point has fanned into a rippling coral of cream, sage, and blush pink, grafted onto a sturdy green stock to keep it strong. The full guide covers the graft that keeps it alive, the bright-shade, soak-and-dry care it wants, and one firm caution: its milky sap is toxic, so it lives up high, out of Weenie’s reach.
