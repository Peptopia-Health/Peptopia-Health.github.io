50 Essential Drugs

Class, use, and the one caution that matters most — no doses, just the essentials.

This page began as a nursing-school NotebookLM list, “Fifty Essential Must-Know Drugs” — cleaned, corrected, and verified into a quick reference, with extra weight on the psych and high-alert drugs of inpatient mental-health work.

Cardiovascular11
DrugClassUsed forWatch for
LisinoprilACE inhibitorHypertension; heart failureDry cough is common; watch potassium and kidney function; angioedema (lip or tongue swelling) is an emergency.
MetoprololBeta blockerRate control in atrial fibrillation; hypertension; heart failureSlow pulse and low blood pressure; never stop abruptly; can mask the warning signs of low blood sugar.
FurosemideLoop diureticFluid overload and pulmonary edema in heart failurePotassium and magnesium loss; track intake/output and daily weights; dehydration and dizziness.
WarfarinAnticoagulant (vitamin K antagonist)Preventing clots in atrial fibrillation, DVT/PE, and mechanical heart valvesBleeding; INR blood tests guide the dose; vitamin K in food and many other drugs change its effect.
AtorvastatinStatinLowering cholesterol; reducing heart attack and stroke riskNew muscle pain or weakness — report it (rare muscle breakdown); liver irritation.
AmlodipineCalcium channel blockerHypertension; anginaAnkle swelling is common; headache and flushing; dizziness from low blood pressure.
DigoxinCardiac glycosideHeart failure; rate control in atrial fibrillationNarrow safety margin — toxicity causes nausea, vision changes, and dangerous rhythms; low potassium raises the risk.
AspirinAntiplatelet (salicylate)Preventing heart attack and stroke; after stent placementStomach bleeding — give with food; report black stools or vomiting blood.
ClopidogrelAntiplatelet (P2Y12 inhibitor)Preventing clots after stents; stroke and heart attack preventionBleeding, especially when combined with aspirin; report unusual bruising.
AdenosineAntiarrhythmicFirst-line treatment for stable SVTGiven as a rapid IV push; a few seconds of flushing, chest pressure, and a sense of doom are expected and pass quickly.
AmiodaroneAntiarrhythmicVentricular arrhythmias; rhythm and rate controlLong-term use can injure the thyroid, lungs, and liver; interacts with many drugs; watch blood pressure and heart rate.
Diabetes4
DrugClassUsed forWatch for
MetforminBiguanide (oral diabetes drug)Type 2 diabetesStomach upset; rare lactic acidosis in kidney failure — often held around contrast-dye studies.
InsulinHormone — basal and bolus typesLowering blood glucose in diabetesLow blood sugar is the key danger — know its signs and treatment; high-alert drug, double-check every dose; rotate injection sites.
GlipizideSulfonylurea (oral diabetes drug)Type 2 diabetesLow blood sugar, especially when meals are skipped; weight gain.
SemaglutideGLP-1 receptor agonistType 2 diabetes; weight managementNausea and vomiting; slows stomach emptying; report severe abdominal pain (pancreatitis warning).
Respiratory4
DrugClassUsed forWatch for
AlbuterolShort-acting beta-2 agonist (rescue inhaler)Quick relief of asthma and COPD bronchospasmTremor and racing heart; needing it more often means control is slipping — report it.
IpratropiumAnticholinergic bronchodilatorCOPD; added therapy in asthma flare-upsDry mouth; keep the spray out of the eyes (can worsen glaucoma).
PrednisoneCorticosteroidInflammation; asthma and COPD flare-ups; autoimmune flaresRaises blood sugar; mood and sleep changes; infection risk; taper after longer courses — never stop suddenly.
MontelukastLeukotriene modifierAsthma maintenance; allergic rhinitisBoxed warning: serious mood and behavior changes — report agitation, depression, or suicidal thoughts.
Psych / Neuro13
DrugClassUsed forWatch for
SertralineSSRI antidepressantDepression; anxiety disorders; PTSD; OCDSerotonin syndrome risk with other serotonergic drugs; low sodium in older adults; takes several weeks to work.
GabapentinGabapentinoid anticonvulsantNerve pain; seizure disordersSedation and dizziness — fall risk; misuse potential; taper rather than stopping suddenly.
PregabalinGabapentinoid anticonvulsantNerve pain; fibromyalgia; seizure disordersSedation, dizziness, and weight gain; controlled substance with misuse potential.
RisperidoneAtypical antipsychoticSchizophrenia; bipolar maniaWeight, blood sugar, and cholesterol rise; movement effects (EPS); raised prolactin; dizziness on standing.
LithiumMood stabilizerBipolar disorderNarrow safety margin — toxicity starts with tremor, nausea, and confusion; blood levels plus kidney and thyroid checks; dehydration and low-salt intake raise levels.
AripiprazoleAtypical antipsychoticSchizophrenia; bipolar disorder; add-on treatment for depressionAkathisia — an inner restlessness; impulse-control problems such as gambling have been reported.
HaloperidolTypical antipsychoticSchizophrenia; severe agitation and psychosisMovement effects and tardive dyskinesia; neuroleptic malignant syndrome (fever plus rigidity) is an emergency; prolongs the QT interval.
ZolpidemSedative-hypnoticShort-term insomniaComplex sleep behaviors (sleep-walking, sleep-driving); falls, especially in older adults; take only when staying in bed.
LorazepamBenzodiazepineAnxiety; agitation; alcohol withdrawal; seizuresSedation and dependence; breathing suppression with opioids or alcohol; fall risk in older adults.
DonepezilCholinesterase inhibitorAlzheimer's dementia — slows symptom progressionNausea and diarrhea; can slow the heart — watch for fainting.
QuetiapineAtypical antipsychoticSchizophrenia; bipolar disorder; add-on treatment for depressionStrong sedation and dizziness on standing; weight, blood sugar, and cholesterol rise.
TrazodoneAtypical antidepressant (SARI)Depression; widely used for insomniaSedation and dizziness on standing; priapism (prolonged, painful erection) is rare but an emergency.
BenztropineAnticholinergicTreats antipsychotic movement effects (acute dystonia, drug-induced parkinsonism)Dry mouth, constipation, and urinary retention; confusion in older adults.
Pain7
DrugClassUsed forWatch for
AcetaminophenNon-opioid analgesic / fever reducerPain and feverLiver damage in overdose — add up the total from ALL products; many combination medicines hide it.
IbuprofenNSAIDPain, inflammation, and feverStomach bleeding and kidney strain; give with food; extra caution with blood thinners.
OxycodoneOpioid analgesicModerate to severe painSlowed breathing and sedation; constipation; dependence risk.
HydromorphoneOpioid analgesicModerate to severe painFar stronger than morphine milligram for milligram — dosing mix-ups are dangerous; slowed breathing.
FentanylSynthetic opioid analgesicSevere painExtremely potent — breathing can slow rapidly; with patches, heat increases absorption.
MorphineOpioid analgesicModerate to severe painSlowed breathing, sedation, and constipation; active breakdown products build up in kidney failure.
NaloxoneOpioid antagonist (reversal agent)Reversing opioid overdoseCan trigger sudden withdrawal in opioid-dependent patients; its effect may wear off before the opioid does — keep watching the patient.
Antibiotics / GI / Other11
DrugClassUsed forWatch for
AzithromycinMacrolide antibioticBacterial respiratory and other infectionsProlongs the QT interval; stomach upset; does nothing for viruses.
CeftriaxoneCephalosporin antibiotic (3rd generation)Broad bacterial coverage — pneumonia, meningitis, and other serious infectionsAsk about penicillin-allergy history; diarrhea, including C. diff risk.
VancomycinGlycopeptide antibioticSerious Gram-positive infections, including MRSAKidney injury — drug levels and kidney function are monitored; infusing too fast causes 'red man syndrome' (flushing and rash).
MetronidazoleAntibacterial and antiprotozoalAnaerobic bacterial and protozoal infections, including C. diffNO alcohol during treatment and for at least 48 hours after — it causes a severe reaction; metallic taste; dark urine is harmless.
OmeprazoleProton pump inhibitor (PPI)Acid reflux (GERD); stomach ulcer protectionTake before meals; long-term use is linked to low magnesium and bone loss; can reduce clopidogrel's effect.
OndansetronAntiemetic (5-HT3 blocker)Nausea and vomitingProlongs the QT interval; headache and constipation.
EnoxaparinLow-molecular-weight heparin (anticoagulant)Preventing and treating DVT and pulmonary embolismBleeding and injection-site bruising; a rare platelet reaction (HIT) — report unusual bruising or new clot symptoms.
LevothyroxineThyroid hormone replacementHypothyroidism (underactive thyroid)Take on an empty stomach at the same time daily; too much causes racing heart and tremor; iron and calcium block its absorption.
Dutasteride5-alpha-reductase inhibitorEnlarged prostate (benign prostatic hyperplasia)Pregnant people must not handle crushed or leaking capsules (risk to a male fetus); it lowers PSA test results — flag it to the provider.
AllopurinolXanthine oxidase inhibitorPreventing gout attacks by lowering uric acidStop and report any rash — rare severe skin reactions; it prevents attacks but does not treat one in progress.
HydrochlorothiazideThiazide diureticHypertension; mild fluid retentionLow potassium and sodium; raises blood sugar and uric acid (can trigger gout); dizziness from dehydration.
Verify before you rely

This is a memory aid, not a dosing guide. Confirm any drug against Med ID and the current formulary before you give it — and when in doubt, ask pharmacy.
