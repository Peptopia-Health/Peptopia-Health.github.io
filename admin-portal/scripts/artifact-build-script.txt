# ARTIFACT BUILD SCRIPT — Tuscarora Arboretum artifact guides
*Set by David, October 25, 2026. Companion to the Peptopia guide build. Trigger phrase: **"Artifact: [brief]"** (the guide trigger "Guide: [topic]" remains for product/vendor guides). Both builds share Peptopia design principles (Outfit/DM Sans, no serif ever, orange headline standard, raised circular buttons with the button confirmation check, one-line footer, emphatic highlight standard); the rules below are the artifact-specific layer.*

## A. Privacy & presentation rules (absolute, every artifact publication)
1. NEVER print or publish David's real name. No owner mentions at all — no "the owner," no "prepared for," no "on the owner's word." The work is impersonal and stands on its own.
2. Findspots are stated in GENERAL terms only — neighborhood or landscape terms (for Artifact No. 1: "Irish Hill" and "along the Tuscarora Creek"). Never a street name, never an exact property address, in text, filenames, or URL slugs.
3. No editions. One guide, living. An authoring date shows (month/year level is fine); no version numbers, no edition history, no corrections logs.
4. No process narrative. Never describe how the artifact was read, dated, researched, or identified; no "how we got here," no withdrawn-claim bookkeeping, no apologies, no meta-caveats about sourcing technique ("every fact is sourced…" — delete that species of sentence). We present findings, proudly and plainly.
5. The confidence table covers the ARTIFACT only: documented facts about it, and labeled inferences about it. Nothing about our steps, our certainty journey, or our errors.

## B. Writing doctrine
6. Fact-based, fascinating storytelling. The subject web: the artifact itself, its designer and maker (the maker is a romance equal to the object — the bottle is a reflection of the maker), the era and its culture, world events and local pressures of the time, the supply chain, the merchant trade, the place. Weave them into one cohesive, enchanting story.
7. Plain language, kind and engaging, easy for a high-school reader with a short attention span; complex without feeling complicated; concise over dictionary-dry. No hedging, no apologies — confident, sourced facts and honest inferences.
8. Inferences are welcome and labeled INFERENCE when they are logical, probable-fact style ("Most bottles in 1937 were machine-made, so this one almost certainly was too"). Production-window statements of that kind are the model.
9. Sources at the end are HYPERLINKED to the public page where each source can be explored further.

## C. Multi-modal build (every artifact, no exceptions)
10. Images are load-bearing: real photographs of the artifact (best 2–3 lead), the patent drawing when one exists, period/context images where they teach. No long text-only runs — from the middle of the guide onward, every stretch is broken by a visual: a photo, a graphic, an icon row, a chart, a popup button.
11. TIMELINE GRAPHIC: a horizontal bar, left to right, tall enough to read at a glance, dates along the bottom, a custom icon at each key date, and each date CLICKABLE — it jumps to the section where that date is explained (and the reader can jump back). Timelines turn dull chronology into a map readers remember.
12. INTERACTIVITY: buttons/popups that reveal discreet points on tap (the Peptopia popup standards), anchor-linked navigation, the patent popup on the dedicated page.
13. AUDIO: a concise brief (2 minutes maximum) in Smooth voice — a fascinating fireside summary, not a data dump. Basics only: what it is, who made it, when, where it fits in history, value if documented. STORYTELLER PERSONA (David, Oct 25, 2026): David's own speaking character — interesting, engaging, captivating; masculine and confident; a deep, intelligent voice that puts listeners at ease and draws them a little closer to hear the story better. Write and pace every brief in that spirit. STORAGE RULE (David, Oct 25, 2026, standing for ALL files): overwrite in place; permanently delete superseded editions/versions — never keep multiple copies of a file unless David specifically requests it.
14. The dedicated page carries: the artifact's best photographs, the full guide, the audio player, and — whenever a patent is located — the patent popup button + the card's third link, per the artifact-entry template in MEMORY.md.

43. THE CURIOSITY RULE — WHERE THE LENS GOES (David, November 21, 2026, PERMANENT): the magnifying-lens closeup (§I.32) is never placed on the prettiest or most average field. It is placed where a human collector would magnify, and a human magnifies where information is densest — where the object departs from its own pattern or where its making and history left evidence: to IDENTIFY (maker's marks, mold seams, pontil/pole scars — construction evidence concentrates at poles, bases, seams); to AUTHENTICATE and DATE (technique clues — cane work, swirl convergence, slag structure — live at convergence points); to ASSESS CONDITION (chips, cracks, bruises, pitting — damage is the highest-curiosity signal; it changes value and story); to EXPLAIN ANOMALIES (color intrusions, inclusions, anywhere the surface breaks its own norm, because deviation carries information); and to VERIFY MATERIAL (the texture — frost, frit, bubbles — that distinguishes one maker's glass from another's). Principle: curiosity = deviation × significance; the lens belongs on the strongest cluster of deviation that is also structurally meaningful. PLACEMENT IS COMPUTED, NOT EYEBALLED: the submission processor runs `~/workspace/tuscarora-arboretum/curiosity_lens.py` on the artifact's clear master as part of the §G/§H image step, and the §I.32 lens closeup is built from the algorithm's chosen point. The scorer slides a window the size of the lens interior across the isolated subject and scores five signals — damage/dark-outlier density (the weightiest), structural convergence (pole/seam proximity and silhouette-edge irregularity), color deviation from the subject's own dominant palette, texture break (local variance and edge-density anomalies), and rarity (the window least like the subject's average window) — combined into one curiosity score per window, ranked under a minimum-separation rule so the leaders are distinct regions. CONDITION PRIORITY: a damage-led candidate within 85% of the top score is chosen over a merely decorative field even at a slightly lower raw score, because condition is what a collector magnifies first. GUARDRAILS: the analysis never alters the subject; the chosen point must sit on the subject with the lens interior on-subject; if the standard lens radius would push the rim off the frame at the chosen point, the radius shrinks to the largest that keeps the whole lens in frame — the center never moves to fit the rim; the interior stays spatially consistent per §I.32; and every run's ranked candidates, per-signal breakdowns, and QA contact sheet are retained in the build record, because the algorithm proposes with its reasoning shown and David can always override it (his word moved the Mint Swirl's lens onto its chipped pole — the judgment this rule exists to reproduce). If the scorer reports a FLAT subject (no strong curiosity peak), the build says so plainly and a human decides; a featureless subject may warrant no lens at all. THE GALAXY REFINEMENT (learned November 21, 2026, validating against Marble No. 1): when the subject's signature feature is a BROAD FIELD spread nearly evenly over the surface (the Galaxy's star-flecks), no interior window out-scores the rim, where the structural and rarity bonuses pile up as artifacts — DISCOUNT rim candidates on such subjects and give convergence and energy centers (a swirl's vortex, a pattern's knot) their doctrinal priority: deviation × significance is measured at the pattern's heart, not its edge. On dark, saturated glass the damage channel also over-reads the object's own dark pattern as damage, and the lens interior's enhancement must be GENTLE (small lifts only) so the magnified view keeps the object's natural palette. David's instinct named the Galaxy's target — "the highly energetic swirl area" — and the rule, correctly applied, chooses the same: the vortex where the swirl converges and pours out.
